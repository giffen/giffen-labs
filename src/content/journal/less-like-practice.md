*Automatic Pocket 4.0 went to Apple for review on November 11. It's free, for iPhone and iPad, on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/automatic-pocket/id6759693937), where 3.0 stays until 4.0 is approved.*

On October 30 I published [a post](/journal/the-scoring-was-the-bug) about turning Automatic Pocket into a bass app. Most of it was about the scoring: how the app was sure I couldn't play, how the fix was a different game, and how I said "Kick scoring is the app now." It ended with me "most evenings, trying to get my best take past 70."

That isn't what my evenings looked like. Eleven days later I moved the kick game behind a button, and the app now opens on a drum machine. This is why.

## What a session actually took

To score a take, you put on headphones, because on the speaker the phone's kick drum covers the note it's asking you to play. You calibrate the headphone delay. On Bluetooth that means holding an earbud against the phone for about 20 seconds while the app fits a trend line, because Bluetooth audio gets later the longer it plays. My amp drifts enough that the results screen told me to take the score with a grain of salt. Then you play, and then you get a number.

I didn't like calibrating, and the whole thing never felt right. The score wasn't wrong, exactly. We'd tuned it against my own takes until it agreed with my ears. It just turned playing along with a drummer into a test.

The night before that post went up, I'd added a Just drums button next to Start: the groove plays, nothing listens, nothing is scored. The post mentioned it as an afterthought, for "some evenings." It turned into most evenings. It was much more fun, and I played for longer. I already knew I play longer to a drum machine than to a metronome. It feels less like practice. The scored session had quietly turned the drum machine back into a metronome with homework.

That's the whole argument for 4.0. A practice app is supposed to get the instrument into your hands for longer. If the score makes you put the bass down sooner, the drums are the better practice tool, however much the score can tell you.

## What 4.0 is

4.0 renames the app Automatic Pocket: Drum Machine. It opens on one Play button. No microphone, no headphones, no setup, and it works on the speaker.

- **36 beats** (there were 10) across rock and pop, funk and soul, hip-hop and electronic, Latin and Caribbean, swing and shuffle, plus bare clicks for slow practice. Any tempo from 40 to 200.
- **Change anything while it plays.** Tempo, beat, what the drummer's doing. The new drums take over on the same beat of the same bar, about an eighth of a second later, so you don't stop playing to try a different groove.
- **Your own beats.** A step grid with ten drums, straight or triplet steps, ghost notes and accents. It loops while you draw. Press and hold any built-in beat to start from a copy.
- **A drummer, not a loop.** This came from me playing with it. I asked for "genre specific fills that you could configure to get added in," and then, "does it make sense to have the drummer switch up the groove from time to time?" So the drummer plays a fill at the end of every 4, 8 or 16 bars, in the beat's own style, never the same one twice running, with a crash on the one after. It can switch to a B section for 8 or 16 bars, usually by moving the time from the hi-hat to the ride, and come back. It still drops out for a few bars so you have to hold the time, and it can drift the tempo for you to follow. Three toms came in with the fills.

One small change I liked: the speaker got louder. To measure timing through the mic, the app ran iOS audio in measurement mode, which costs the speaker a lot of its level. Drums on their own don't need to measure anything, so they play in the normal mode now. The scoring had been making the drums quieter the whole time.

The kick game is still there, behind Score my timing. Put on headphones, play a note on every kick, get the needle and the score. Nothing about it got worse. It just isn't the front door anymore.

## Testing it isn't using it

The easy lesson is "eat your own dog food." I did, but that doesn't explain anything, because I was eating it both times. "Kick scoring is the app now" came from playing it every night. So did "drums first."

The difference was why I was playing. In late October I was testing. I played takes so the scoring could be tuned, and four of them became test fixtures. That work was useful; the kick game is better for it. But testing has a goal, and the goal was the score, so of course the score looked like the app.

After 3.0 shipped I was just practising. Nobody was tuning anything. And what I reached for, night after night, was the button that didn't score me. Eating your own dog food to check it isn't the same as eating it because you're hungry. The second kind is the one that tells you what the product is.

## What Claude did, and what I did

Claude Opus 5.5, in Claude Code, built 4.0 over two days: the 36 beats, the editor, the live hand-off between beats (unit-tested to the sample), the fills, the toms, the B sections, the louder speaker, the App Store listing, the new screenshots and the review notes.

It couldn't have made the call. Nothing in the code said the kick game was the wrong front door. The tests passed, the scores matched my ears, and the app did what the post said it did. The signal was which button I pressed at night when I wasn't working on it, and only I could see that. Then the drummer features came from playing with the drums the way you play with a person: after a while you want them to fill, and to change it up.

## One user, again

The last post admitted the grade was calibrated against one bassist, a beginner, me. This time the whole app is. As far as I know, I'm also its only user. I made something I like, and that's a real bar. But it says nothing yet about whether anyone else wants a drum machine that behaves like a drummer.

So here's what I'll watch. The app collects no data, so all I have is the App Store: downloads, and ratings. 4.0 asks for a rating once per version, after your third play of three minutes or more. That's after you've played long enough to know. If people play past that point and rate it, I'm onto something. If they don't, being user number one told me what to cut, and not what other people want.

For now it's a drummer, a bass, and me, playing longer than I meant to.
